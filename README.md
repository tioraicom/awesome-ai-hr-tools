58 tools for an HR function, grouped by the working year rather than by the software market:
the advert, the search, the record, the logistics, the interview, the first ninety days, the
review cycle, the training budget, the payroll run, and the headcount question.

One rule decides what is here, and it costs the list several large products. A tool may prepare
a judgement about a person; it may not make one. Anything whose purpose is to score a named
candidate from material nobody can review is absent, and the methodology says so rather than
leaving a quiet gap where those products would be.

Maintained by [TiorAI](https://tiorai.com/), which catalogues AI tools for a living.

## Job adverts and employer messaging

The words that reach a candidate before anybody has read a CV.

- **[Appcast](https://www.appcast.io/)** — Buys job advertising by the application received rather than by the click. `Paid` `Web`
- **[Datapeople](https://datapeople.io/)** — Flags wording in a job post that narrows who applies, while the post is still open. `Paid` `Web` `Browser Extension`
- **[Joveo](https://www.joveo.com/)** — Moves advertising budget between job boards according to what each one returns. `Paid` `Web`
- **[Ongig](https://www.ongig.com/)** — Rewrites job descriptions to a house style and checks them against an exclusion list. `Paid` `Web`
- **[Sense](https://www.sensehq.com/)** — Keeps a candidate pipeline warm with scheduled messages across email and text. `Paid` `Web` `API`
- **[Talkpush](https://www.talkpush.com/)** — Runs first contact as a chat on the channel the candidate already uses. `Paid` `Web`
- **[Textio](https://textio.com/)** — Shows how a phrase has performed in real hiring language before you send it. `Paid` `Web` `Browser Extension`

## Sourcing and candidate search

Finding people who have not applied. Retrieval, not evaluation.

- **[Fetcher](https://fetcher.ai/)** — Builds a shortlist from a written brief and sends the first email for you. `Paid` `Web`
- **[Gem](https://www.gem.com/)** — Treats candidates as a CRM, so an old conversation is findable two years later. `Paid` `Web` `Browser Extension`
- **[hireEZ](https://hireez.com/)** — Searches public profiles across sites and merges what it finds into one record. `Paid` `Web` `Browser Extension`
- **[Juicebox](https://juicebox.ai/)** — Takes a search written as a sentence instead of a boolean string. `Freemium` `Web`
- **[Loxo](https://loxo.co/)** — Combines sourcing, contact details, and the agency pipeline in one product. `Paid` `Web`
- **[SeekOut](https://www.seekout.com/)** — Indexes technical and clinical profiles that general search tends to miss. `Paid` `Web`

## Applicant tracking and hiring workflow

The system of record: where an application lives and who has seen it.

- **[Ashby](https://www.ashbyhq.com/)** — Puts reporting in the same product as the pipeline rather than in a separate tool. `Paid` `Web` `API`
- **[Greenhouse](https://www.greenhouse.com/)** — Built around structured interviews, with scorecards defined before the first call. `Paid` `Web` `API`
- **[Lever](https://www.lever.co/)** — Keeps sourcing outreach and inbound applications in a single pipeline. `Paid` `Web` `API`
- **[Manatal](https://www.manatal.com/)** — Priced for small teams and agencies rather than for an enterprise rollout. `Paid` `Web` `API`
- **[Recruitee](https://recruitee.com/)** — Designed so hiring managers work in it, not only recruiters. `Paid` `Web` `API`
- **[Teamtailor](https://www.teamtailor.com/)** — Ships a career site builder alongside the tracking system. `Paid` `Web` `API`
- **[Workable](https://www.workable.com/)** — Covers posting, tracking, offers, and onboarding without a second subscription. `Paid` `Web` `iOS` `Android` `API`
- **[Zoho Recruit](https://www.zoho.com/recruit/)** — Has a free single-user tier, which is rare in this category. `Freemium` `Web` `iOS` `Android` `API`

## Scheduling and candidate communication

- **[GoodTime](https://goodtime.io/)** — Arranges panel interviews where a dozen calendars have to agree. `Paid` `Web`
- **[Grayscale](https://www.grayscaleapp.com/)** — Built for hourly hiring at volume, where text messages beat email. `Paid` `Web`

- **[Prelude](https://www.prelude.co/)** — Handles interview logistics as a workload, room booking and rescheduling included. `Paid` `Web`

## Interviews and work-sample assessment

Where the evidence rule bites hardest. Every entry here shows its working.

- **[BrightHire](https://brighthire.com/)** — Records the interview so a hiring decision can be checked against what was said. `Paid` `Web`
- **[CoderPad](https://coderpad.io/)** — A shared editor for a live technical interview, with the session replayable. `Freemium` `Web`
- **[CodeSignal](https://codesignal.com/)** — Scores a program against tests that the candidate and the reviewer can both read. `Paid` `Web`
- **[Codility](https://www.codility.com/)** — Runs timed programming tasks and keeps the submitted code for review. `Paid` `Web`
- **[HackerRank](https://www.hackerrank.com/)** — A large question bank with the candidate's answers stored beside the result. `Freemium` `Web`
- **[Metaview](https://www.metaview.ai/)** — Writes the interview notes so the interviewer can listen instead of typing. `Freemium` `Web`

- **[Lattice](https://lattice.com/)** — Ties goals, reviews, and one-to-ones together instead of running them separately. `Paid` `Web`

## How tools are selected

A tool qualifies when an **HR function buys it and operates it**: writing the advert, finding
the candidate, running the process, keeping the record, paying the person, training them, or
measuring the organisation they work in. It has to be sold to HR rather than borrowed by it,
and it has to pass the evidence rule below.

### A tool may prepare a judgement about a person. It may not make one

This is the rule that decides what is here, and it costs this list several large, well-funded
products. It is stated first because a reader deserves to know what was left out before they
notice the gap.

A model will rank forty applicants in a second, in fluent prose, with reasoning nobody can
check. The candidate cannot see the reasoning, the recruiter cannot test it, and the company
cannot explain it to the person who was rejected or to a tribunal that asks. That is not a
tooling preference. It is the difference between a decision a person made with help and a
decision a system made and a person signed.

So the line is drawn at **evidence**, not at automation:

- **Listed:** anything that produces material a human then judges. A transcript. A structured
  note. A search result. A submitted program and the tests it passed.
- **Not listed:** anything whose purpose is to output a score, grade, rank, or hire signal about
  a **named person** from material that cannot be reviewed. Automated video-interview scoring,
  personality or culture-fit inference from voice, face, or writing, and CV ranking that returns
  a fitness number are all out.

Three consequences are worth stating plainly, because each one looks like an inconsistency
until you apply the rule.

**A coding test is listed and an interview scorer is not**, although both produce a number
about a candidate. The coding test's evidence is the candidate's own submitted program, and
anybody can read it, run it, and disagree with the mark. Nothing a video scorer looked at can be
read by anyone.

**Sourcing tools are listed**, because retrieval is not evaluation. A search that returns people
matching a query does what a boolean search does, faster. What would be excluded is the
product that then tells you which of them to hire.

**An applicant tracking system is listed for its record-keeping**, and several of them now ship
an optional ranking feature. They are here because a system of record is what they are for, and
the rule is about what a product is for. If a vendor repositions the ranking as the product,
the entry comes out.

The same rule applies after the hire. A performance tool may draft from evidence a manager
supplies; a tool that generates the assessment itself is the same problem with a longer
employment relationship attached.

### What the categories are telling you

The ten groups follow the working year of an HR function rather than the software market: the
advert, the search, the record, the logistics, the interview, the first ninety days, the review
cycle, the training budget, the payroll run, and the headcount question.

- **Sourcing** and **Applicant tracking** are separate because several vendors sell both and
  are good at one. Buying a tracking system for its sourcing is the common mistake here.
- **Interviews** holds two different things on purpose: products that record what was said, and
  products that set a work sample. They share a category because they share the evidence rule.
- **Payroll, benefits, and global employment** is where the AI content is thinnest and the
  operational stakes are highest. It is included because an HR stack without it is not a stack.

### What is excluded

Automated interview scoring, personality and culture-fit inference, and CV ranking that
returns a fitness number, for the reason given above. Anything that infers a protected
characteristic, an emotion, or a psychological trait from a face, a voice, or a writing sample.
Background-check and social-media screening products, which are a different regulated industry.

Also out: recruitment agencies and outsourced interviewing, which are a service with a
dashboard; candidate-side products such as CV builders and interview coaching, which have a
different reader; general office software an HR team happens to use; and anything in closed
beta or on a waitlist.

Entries are ordered alphabetically inside each category. Position carries no meaning, and
there is no paid placement, sponsorship, or affiliate link anywhere in this repository.

## Disclaimer

Everything listed is built and operated by a third party, not by TiorAI. Pricing, plan
limits, and terms change without notice, and a tool that fits today may not next quarter.

**Automated decisions about people are regulated, and the regulation is moving.** The EU AI
Act classes employment and worker-management systems as high risk; New York City requires a
bias audit and candidate notice for automated employment decision tools; several other
jurisdictions have their own rules and more are drafting them. A tool being listed here is not
advice that deploying it where you are is lawful, and the evidence rule this repository applies
is an editorial standard, not a legal one. Take advice before you automate any part of a hiring
or a firing.

**Nothing here is scored on fairness or accuracy.** Vendor bias-audit summaries are published
by the vendor, cover the vendor's own configuration, and cannot be reproduced from outside the
product. This list does not repeat them and does not rank anything on them. Ask for the full
audit, ask who ran it, and ask what population it was run on.

**Employee data is personal data, usually of the most sensitive kind.** Several categories here
hold salary, performance, health-adjacent, and grievance records. Check retention, residency,
and access controls before you connect any of them to your payroll or your directory.

Listing here is editorial selection, not endorsement.
